Stage 2 test

Playtest guide

Thank you for testing. This page tells you what to install, what to try and what to tell me.

About 20 minutes. You need the game on your computer or Steam Deck, and ideally one or two friends. Please do not read the Mechanics page first: one of the questions is whether a new player understands the game without any explanation.

What this test is about

Version 0.4 adds combat and a second mode, Defend the Altar: you train in a first room, then defend a shared altar from three waves of enemies together. Courier Route is still there. The test is built to answer these questions:

  • Is combat readable: can you tell what the enemy will do, where it will hit and when to roll?
  • Do you choose between attacking and stepping back, or do you just swing?
  • Does helping a downed friend feel worth it, and does the team need each other?
  • Is the altar fight too easy or too hard (the numbers are first guesses)?

There is no art and almost no sound: the level is made of coloured blocks on purpose.

Before you start

  1. Install the game with the user guide (Mac, Steam Deck or Windows). Everyone in a session must be on exactly the same version; it is written at the top of the main menu.
  2. Pick how you will play: keyboard and mouse, or a controller. If you can, try the controller for one whole round, menus included.
  3. For playing together, one person chooses Host a game and, in the pause menu (Esc or Start), Invite friends shows the addresses to send. The others choose Join a game.

What to do

  1. Defend the Altar, alone (about 10 minutes). Choose the mode Defend the Altar (the mode button in the menu) and Solo. Do not read anything first. Hit the targets in the first room (green is a point, red costs one), watch the danger zones flash before they hurt, then stand on the cyan ready zone to open the door. Reach the blue defence zone near the altar before the first wave. Try the roll (Space or B) just as an enemy winds up its strike. Note when you were confused.
  2. Defend the Altar, together (about 15 minutes). One hosts, the others join (everyone picks the same mode, or joins and is moved by itself). Beat all three waves. Try to get a friend up when they are down (hold E or A next to them for four seconds), and see what happens if you all go down or the altar falls.
  3. Courier Route (about 5 minutes). Quickly check it still works.
  4. Alone, without help (about 5 minutes). Choose Solo and try to work out what to do. Do not read anything. Notice every moment you were unsure what to do next, and what finally told you.
  5. Together (about 10 minutes). One hosts, the others join. Complete the route together at least twice. After the first time, the host presses Play again (or Restart run in the pause menu) and you go again without anyone leaving.
  6. Try to break it (about 5 minutes). Fall into the red strips with an item. Drop an item in odd places. Leave the session and join again in the middle of a run. Grab the same item at the same time. Try to pick things up through a wall (it should not work). Drop balls on the green pad and take them out again, throw one (hold the Throw button, R or RB, for half a second; let go earlier and nothing happens), watch the skills at the bottom left, where the ball lands and whether it moves evenly when you watch another player throw it, and throw one off the edge of the map. Press buttons in the menus with a controller only. Tell us what happened.

Known limits

  • Nobody but the developer has played the combat or Defend the Altar yet: the damage, the speeds, the timers (15 s preparation, 10 s between waves) and the 300 health of the altar are first guesses.
  • There is almost no sound and no art: that is on purpose for now. A downed player is not drawn lying down; they pulse grey-red.
  • There is no separate results screen: victory and defeat are a large message, and the pause menu restarts the run.
  • Typing an address needs a keyboard; with a controller use the list of recent hosts.
  • How the menus feel on the Steam Deck in Gaming Mode, and the Windows launcher, have had little testing.
  • Nothing is saved after a run: no best times.
