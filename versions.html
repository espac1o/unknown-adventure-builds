Version history

Versions

What each version added, fixed and left open, with a link to its release. Newest first.

Everyone playing together must be on exactly the same version. To update, open the launcher and press Enter (see the user guide); older versions can be installed again from the menu.

v0.3.11

2026-11-07Latest

A client now sees health, effects and the ball update every tick instead of ten times a second, and you can switch the statistics overlay and an opt-in playtest recording on from the menu.

What's new
  • Statistics: on/off in the main and pause menus, so the overlay (frames, timings, the connection) works on a Steam Deck and with a controller too. F3 still works, and the choice is remembered.
  • Share playtest data: on/off (off by default). While it is on, each device records a timeline of its session (frame times, hits, stun, push and boost, health changes, who holds the ball) and sends the file to the developer when the session ends. No names, no addresses, only a random id per run. The files of all players of one session can be laid on one timeline to compare the host and a client.
Fixed
  • Health on a client fell in steps of about three (97, 94, 91) while the host saw every point. The network layer sent every synchronised value at most ten times a second by default; the hazard deals 25 a second, so each update carried about 2.5 points. Everything now goes out every tick (30 a second). This also makes the ball and the effects of a hit arrive sooner on a client.
Under the hood

Analytics 2.0.0 (new: timeline recording, session files, upload to a small server). Netcode analysis in NETCODE_ANALYSIS.md. Tests: 205 unit and 24 play-mode.

Known limits

Not checked on real devices. A hit on a client can still show a little later than on the host (the trip over the network plus smoothing); the numbers to look at are in the F3 overlay and, with data sharing on, in the recorded timeline. Please play a session with Share playtest data on, with the hit and the health loss in it.

v0.3.10

2026-11-07Latest

A hit pushes and stuns the person at once on every screen, and a client who comes back is put on the respawn point without first showing the place of death. The timing no longer relies on a guessed network delay.

What's new
  • Net line in F3. The overlay shows your ping and how many steps your input runs ahead of the server; on a host, for each other player, how many steps their input runs behind. Please send these numbers if something still feels late.
Fixed
  • A hit on a client took about three seconds to show (and half a second to a second on the host when a client hit them). The server started the push and the stun at a moment it had to guess from the delay of that player's input, and the guess is wrong when the connection varies. Now they start on the very next step of the person hit, and other players' screens start the push when they hear of it.
  • A client who came back first stood at the death place, then jumped to the respawn point and was held a second time. The return moment was counted with the same guess; it is now counted in the player's own input steps, and if a player's input stops altogether the server brings them back by its own clock.
Under the hood

Units 4.0.0 (IUnitMover.inputTick replaces inputTickLag; the respawn plan is counted in the unit's input steps). The automatic test now includes a host who plays: hits both ways at 40 and 100 ms, and a respawn while the delay changes between 30 and 250 ms. Tests: 197 unit and 24 play-mode.

Known limits

Not checked on real devices: the tests run on one computer with a simulated delay, and the cause was found by reasoning, not by reproducing your connection. A client watching the host will always see a hit a little later than the host does (the round trip plus smoothing). Please check hits between the client and the host both ways and a client's respawn.

v0.3.9

2026-11-07Latest

A client and the server now agree on the moment of coming back, being hit and the Ready? start. Ready? lasts two seconds, and a player who is down goes black and fades away, then fades in at the respawn point.

What's new
  • Ready? is two seconds, then Go! for one. The player stands on the respawn point for the whole of Ready? on every screen.
  • Dissolve. A player who is down is black for a second, then fades away over two; on coming back they fade in during the first second of Ready? (a placeholder for something nicer).
  • Overlay (F3). CPU shows the main thread and the render thread without the wait for the screen; draw calls say when they are not available in this kind of build; garbage is shown as growth per second and collections per second.
Fixed
  • A client could move during Ready? and a player who was hit by a ball got pushed late. A player's inputs reach the server a few ticks late, so the moments the server counted (when you come back, when a freeze starts, when a stun or a push begins) were about 0.4 seconds off from your own screen. All of them are now counted on the player's own input timeline, so your screen and the server agree.
  • A respawned player at the death place on other screens. The respawn point is now kept and every screen puts the body there for the whole of Ready?, however late it hears of the respawn.
  • The ball flew through the person hit on the thrower's screen and jumped to them later. It now stops at them, as it does on the server.
Under the hood

Interaction 0.4.1, Units 3.3.0 (the respawn plan is given in the unit's input timeline; a placement window every peer uses; IUnitMover.inputTickLag), GameUI 0.1.2, Objectives 0.2.0, IsoCamera 0.2.0. New checks: a client who keeps a key held through a restart stands still on their own screen, the pushed player sees the push on their own screen, the ball stops at the person on the thrower's screen. Tests: 197 unit and 24 play-mode.

Known limits

Checked only in the automatic test (with 40 to 60 ms of delay added). Please check on the two devices: coming back after a fall, being hit by a ball as a client and as the host, a restart, and whether the two seconds of Ready? feel right. The size of the tick offset varies with the connection, so a connection with a lot of jitter may still show a small step.

v0.3.8

2026-11-07Latest

The throw rules are stricter and clearer, a hit pushes the player and drops the ball where it hit, coming back starts with Ready? and Go!, runs restart after a countdown, and the game no longer works the machine at full load for nothing.

What's new
  • Throw rules. The throw does nothing while it recharges. Let go before a fifth of the charge (0.4 seconds) and nothing is thrown; the ball goes back as it was. The charge circle is yellow until letting go would throw, then green. E (A) puts the ball down again.
  • Hit. The ball stops where it hit and drops there; the person hit is pushed a little the way it flew, then stunned for a second. The boost lasts 1 second.
  • Ready? and Go! A player who comes back stands still for a second (Ready?), then has a second of Go! A new run is announced first: Run restarting... for three seconds, in which the run cannot be finished.
  • Frame limit and F3. Vertical sync is on and the game draws less in the background. F3 shows frames per second, CPU and GPU time, garbage and memory.
Fixed
  • Fans and heat. Vertical sync was off and nothing limited the frame rate, so the game drew as many frames as the machine could, which kept the processor and the graphics card at full load (hot, loud) for a picture that looked the same as at 60. On the playtest Mac the fans no longer start.
  • The drawn body glided from the death point after a respawn, because the smoothing of the drawn body had no limit on a jump. It now follows at once.
Under the hood

Interaction 0.4.0. Tests: 197 unit and 24 play-mode.

Known limits

The numbers (the fifth of the charge, 1 second of boost, the push of 0.6 metres, one second each of Ready? and Go!) are first guesses. Respawn timing between a client and the server was still off in this version; see 0.3.9.

v0.3.7

2026-11-07Latest

Throwing is charged and has consequences: a ball that hits a player stuns them, and a player who is quick enough catches it. Menus come back to a controller after a click, and a player who respawns is shown on the respawn point at once.

What's new
  • Charged throw (R / RB). A tap lets go of the ball (it keeps your speed); this replaces the Drop button, and E only picks up now. Hold the button to charge, up to 2 seconds, and let go to throw: the longer you held, the harder it flies. While you charge you walk slower, your colour darkens a little and the ball sinks and is drawn back, for everyone to see.
  • Hit. A thrown ball that reaches another player stuns them for 1 second and gives the thrower a speed boost as strong as the charge (up to +60% for 3 seconds).
  • Catch (E / A). Press it just before the ball arrives (up to 0.4 seconds) and you catch it and get the boost instead. It can be used again after 1.2 seconds. The skill bar has a Catch slot.
  • Menus: after a click outside the buttons, an arrow, WASD, the d-pad or the stick selects an item again.
Fixed
  • Menu focus lost after a click. A click or touch outside the buttons cleared the selection, and then no key or stick did anything. The next navigation press now selects the item that was selected last (or the top one), without also moving on.
  • Respawn on your own screen. Your character stayed on the place where you died until the server's message that you were back arrived, and kept walking there if you held a direction. The server now sends the respawn point and tick ahead, and your own predicted character is placed there on that tick. In the automatic test your character reaches the respawn point 0.01 to 0.02 seconds after the server does, with 30 to 250 ms of delay (it took 0.21 to 0.45 seconds before).
  • The ball stayed in the thrower's hands until the server answered. It now leaves the hand at once on the thrower's screen on a flight of its own and hands over to the server's ball when both have stopped; the server moves the ball on by the time the request took. With 40 ms of delay and 3% loss the ball is a metre from the hand 81 ms after letting go.
Under the hood

Interaction 0.3.0, Units 3.2.0, GameUI 0.1.2, Objectives 0.2.0, IsoCamera 0.2.0. The stun and the boost are replicated as ticks so the owner's prediction knows when they end; the charge is part of the predicted input, so the server walks as the owner does. Tests: 195 unit, 24 play-mode (including new scenarios for the respawn timing, the charge, the hit, the stun, the boost and the catch) and 74 installer checks.

Known limits

Nobody but the developer has played it yet. The numbers (charge time, speeds, 1 second of stun, +60% boost, catch window of 0.4 seconds and cooldown of 1.2) are first guesses for the playtest: tell us if the stun is too long, the boost too strong or the catch too hard or too easy. The ball does not yet knock the stunned player's own ball out of their hands.

v0.3.5

2026-11-07Latest

A dropped or thrown ball now moves evenly on the other players' screens, not only on the host's.

What's new
  • Nothing new to play: this build fixes how a falling, rolling or thrown ball looks to a player who is not the host.
Fixed
  • A ball was drawn in portions on a client. The host drew it evenly; a client chased the latest position it had received (30 times a second), so any uneven arrival showed as stop-and-go motion, for a ball falling when dropped and for a throw alike. Now every update carries the ball's position, speed and the server tick, and a client plays a short buffer back about 100 milliseconds behind real time, interpolating between the updates around that moment. A late or lost update changes nothing on screen.
  • The last position of a ball could be lost. A change made right after the network had sent that moment's state was not sent again once the ball lay still (a ball that fell off the map could stay drawn in mid-air on other screens). Every change is now sent again for three more ticks.
Under the hood

Interaction 0.2.2, GameUI 0.1.1, Objectives 0.2.0, IsoCamera 0.2.0. A new automatic check throws and drops a ball three times over a network with 40 ms delay and 3% loss and compares how evenly a second client draws it with the server (the thrown ball: 0.14 against 0.45 before, lower is smoother). Other players now see a ball about 100 ms later than before; the delay is a setting on the ball. Tests: 188 unit, 22 play-mode and 74 installer checks.

Known limits

Measured only in the automatic test, with delay and loss added to a local connection; please look at a thrown and a dropped ball on the other player's screen on real devices (Mac and Steam Deck). The 100 ms delay is a trade-off and can be tuned.

v0.3.4

2026-11-07Latest

Throwing is a channelled skill on its own button, with a skill bar at the bottom left that shows each skill, its button, its recharge and its channel.

What's new

  • The skill bar at the bottom left shows Pulse and Throw as rectangles with their names and the button under each. While one recharges it is covered by a radial sweep with the seconds left; while Throw is channelled it shows a radial progress and the status Throwing, with no seconds. Throw is dimmed when you carry nothing.
  • Drop shows as a rectangle there while you carry a ball, and the centre of the screen only shows the Pick up hint next to the ball.
  • The controls strip at the bottom is gone: the skills show their own buttons.
Fixed
  • Letting go of the throw button early used to be a drop in 0.3.3 (a tap dropped, a hold threw). Now a press of Interact drops and the throw button only throws.
Under the hood

Interaction 0.2.1 (adds the throw cooldown length for the bar), GameUI 0.1.1, Objectives 0.2.0, IsoCamera 0.2.0. New pieces for more skills later: a tested channel rule (ChannelGesture), a skill view the bar draws, and a component that describes each skill. Automatic tests: 188 unit tests, 22 play-mode tests and 74 installer checks.

Known limits

The skill bar has been tested in code but not looked at on a screen by anyone but the developer, and the screenshots on the site still show the older HUD with the controls strip. Skills have no icons yet (the name stands in); movement hints are gone, so the pause key (Esc, Start) is no longer shown.

v0.3.3

2026-11-07Latest

The green pad counts the balls lying on it, balls roll and can be thrown, checkpoints have flags, and a few bugs from the third playtest are fixed. The launcher downloads faster.

What's new
  • The pad counts, you decide when to drop. There is no Deliver press any more: a ball that lies on the green pad counts, and picking it up again counts it out, so balls can be put in and taken out. A held ball never counts.
  • Balls are physical. A dropped ball falls, bounces and rolls to a stop; dropped while you walk it keeps half your speed and rolls on.
  • Throwing. Hold Interact for half a second to throw (about 6 metres the way you face); a tap only drops. The throw recharges for 5 seconds; the hint shows it.
  • Checkpoint flags. Each checkpoint has a flag and a border: grey and low ahead, green and raised when it is the active one, dark green once passed.
  • A ball that leaves the map goes back to where it started.
Fixed
  • The unit flickered between two places after a restart or revive while other players moved (only on your own screen). FishNet clients kept their own history of past states, which still held the old position. They no longer do.
  • A checkpoint was reached from inside the red strips. It used a round area around its middle that reached into them. It is now reached by stepping onto its pad.
  • The Join screen could not be used with a controller: the address field kept the controller to itself. Up, down, the stick or B now leave it.
  • A dead unit slows down instead of stopping at once.
Under the hood

Interaction 0.2.0, GameUI 0.1.1, Objectives 0.2.0, IsoCamera 0.2.0. The launcher fetches the archive in four parts at once (GitHub limits each connection: 32 MB took 18 seconds instead of about three minutes in a measurement), prints how long the download, the check and the unpacking took, and, on a Steam Deck in Gaming Mode, shows a progress window when zenity is installed. Automatic tests: 176 unit tests, 19 play-mode tests and 74 installer checks.

Known limits

Not seen on a screen yet by anyone but the developer: the look of the thrown ball, the hold bar and the checkpoint flags. The Windows launcher change (parallel download) has not been run on Windows, and the Steam Deck progress window has not been seen on a Deck. Players walk through balls and cannot kick them.

v0.3.2

2026-11-07Latest

Smoother movement: the character no longer trembles, stops softly, and the camera follows like a spring instead of halting dead. The hint no longer shakes.

What's new
  • The character speeds up and slows down over a fraction of a second instead of starting and stopping at once.
  • The camera eases in and out and never overshoots.
Fixed
  • The character trembled while moving. An extra smoothing step I had added in 0.3.1 fought the network object's own smoothing, so the character stepped along with the network ticks. I removed it; in the test scene the drawn character is now 0.0 mm off a straight line (it was 39 mm).
  • The stop was too hard. The character went from full speed to zero in one step and the camera stopped with it. The character now takes about 0.15 s to stop and the camera settles in about half a second.
  • The hint shook. It was placed before the camera moved each frame. It is now placed after the camera and the character have moved.
  • Other players. Their movement is drawn with a short fixed delay (three network ticks) so it is even.
Under the hood

IsoCamera 0.2.0 (the camera follows as a critically damped spring, smooth time 0.16 s). The character accelerates at 45 and decelerates at 32 metres per second squared. Automatic tests: 154 unit tests and 18 play-mode tests, including a smoothness check in the multiplayer test scene.

Known limits

Checked by the developer on one computer; please playtest on every device. Other players' movement may still show a brief hitch now and then (seen once in three automatic runs; the cause is not proven).

v0.3.0

2026-11-03Latest

The first playable route: Courier Route. Collect three items, carry them past hazards, hand them in together, with menus that work on a controller. This is the Stage 1 playtest build.

What's new
  • Courier Route: a route with a start pad, a pickup room, a hazard field and a goal; the objective is to hand in three items together.
  • Items: pick up, carry one at a time, drop or deliver, with a pulsing target and a hint ([E] / [A]); held and dropped items go in front of the way you face.
  • Facing: units turn toward the way they move (a dark nose shows it).
  • Health and hazards: the red strips hurt; at zero you are down, drop what you carry and come back at the latest checkpoint after three seconds.
  • Shared objective, timer and results screen; restart the run without leaving the session.
  • New camera that glides after the player and cuts away walls in the way.
  • New menus for mouse and controller (main menu, join, pause, results), a HUD, and a status bar at the bottom right that never shows old information.
Fixed
  • The connection status no longer stays on screen. After leaving a session, Disconnected stayed at the top even when you started or joined another one. It now lives in a status bar at the bottom right, shows only the current session and disappears by itself.
Under the hood

Units 3.1.0, plus new libraries for the camera, interaction, objectives and the interface (not yet published separately). Automatic tests: 135 unit tests and 18 play-mode tests, including three runs in a row, a player joining mid-run and a virtual controller driving the menus.

Known limits

This is a test build: the route has not been played by anyone but its developer. The damage rate, the pick-up range and the camera are first guesses. Typing an address needs a keyboard; the Steam Deck menus and the Windows launcher need your feedback.

v0.2.3

2026-11-03Latest

The Windows launcher: one command installs and updates the game, and a Start menu entry starts it. The game itself did not change.

What's new
  • Windows installer and launcher. Paste one line into PowerShell (or double-click UnknownAdventure-installer.cmd) and the game installs, a Start menu entry named Unknown Adventure appears, and every start checks for a new version first. It works offline, keeps the previous version for going back, and checks every download for size and hash, like the Mac and Steam Deck launchers.
  • The zip for Windows is still published.
Fixed

No gameplay changes in this version.

Under the hood

The installer is a PowerShell script with the same manifest, checks, rollback and menu as the Mac and Linux one. The project now includes the DI container and VContainer (with an injector for networked objects), a test environment (31 unit tests and 9 play-mode tests, run with Tools/test.sh), and the reactive libraries moved from UniRx to R3. None of this changes how the game plays.

Known limits

The Windows launcher and the Windows build have not been run on a Windows PC yet, and the launcher is untested on many setups. A session hosted from a Steam Deck is untested.

Interface polish and a fix for the shared ball. The connection panel can be hidden, the controls are always visible, and your last three hosts are remembered.

What's new
